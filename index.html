// case study

MANTRA’s canonical stMANTRA liquid staking

The official stMANTRA system, with a safe unbonding path
MANTRA mainnet · StakingVault · DelegationManager · NodeOperatorRegistry · WithdrawalManager

Problem. MANTRA needed its canonical stMANTRA liquid-staking system to offer a clean deposit/withdraw experience on top of native staking precompiles and their protocol-enforced unbonding delay. Funds are locked while unbonding, so withdrawals must be honored fairly and without stranding value.

Approach. An ERC-4626 StakingVault whose underlying asset() is wrapped native (WETH), issuing stMANTRA shares. It accepts native OM through a payable receive() — wrapping it to WETH — or WETH directly via deposit(), minting shares and adding the stake to a pending queue. A DelegationManager routes pending stake to the validator set tracked by a NodeOperatorRegistry. A dedicated WithdrawalManager isolates the hard part: it queues withdrawal requests, initiates unbonding against the precompile, and lets users claim once the unbonding window clears — keeping exit accounting off the vault's hot path.

receive() external payable; // wrap native OM → WETH, mint stMANTRA

Trade-offs. Splitting the WithdrawalManager and DelegationManager out of the vault adds contracts and cross-contract calls, but keeps deposit/withdraw logic auditable and confines unbonding-state complexity to one place. Native wrapping/unwrapping avoids a separate wrapper token and its trust assumptions, at the cost of tight coupling to precompile semantics — covered with extensive integration tests. All contracts ship behind upgradeable proxies to leave room for the staking model to evolve.

Outcome. All four contracts deployed as upgradeable proxies to MANTRA mainnet and testnet. The StakingVault is MANTRA Chain’s canonical stMANTRA contract, backed by an end-to-end TypeScript/Hardhat suite exercising the full deposit → delegate → unbond → claim lifecycle and its edge cases.
